\begin{resumo}
\begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
This dissertation evaluates a multi-agent system based on four multimodal large language models (Gemini-3.1-flash-lite, GPT-5.4-nano, Qwen3-VL, and MedGemma 1.5) for binary melanoma-versus-non-melanoma classification in dermoscopic images, comparing it to a supervised CNN (EfficientNetV2-S). The system's three components -- an automatic segmentation model (selected via nested cross-validation on ISIC2018 Task 1), the CNN (trained and threshold-calibrated on BCN20000), and the multi-agent system itself (three diagnostic agents -- ABCD, Menzies, and the Seven-Point Checklist -- plus a summarizer agent, with no critical/retrieval-augmented agent) -- were developed independently and brought together only at evaluation time, over a frozen sample of 3,000 HAM10000 lesions plus a complementary subset of 300. The quantitative evaluation compared five metrics (accuracy, precision, sensitivity, specificity, and F1) across six analyses -- CNN versus the multi-agent system; multi-agent organization versus the same model in a single call (direct-VLM); and the effects of the reference and predicted contours -- using Wilson confidence intervals and paired, stratified bootstrap. The findings do not point in a single direction: the CNN, transferred from BCN20000 to HAM10000, retained high specificity (89.90\%) with moderate sensitivity (51.01\%), consistent with -- though not isolated as the cause of -- a domain shift between the two datasets; multi-agent organization improved Gemini-3.1-flash-lite's accuracy, precision, specificity, and F1 over the direct-VLM condition, but at the cost of sensitivity; the segmentation contour produced no detectable conditional difference in most comparisons, yet measurably reduced binary-decision coverage; and MedGemma 1.5 showed the lowest conditional accuracy among the locally run models, linked to a high proportion of out-of-format responses. The multi-agent synthesis fidelity review (300 lesions $\times$ 4 models, ten criteria, with a human sampling check finding no disagreements) showed that textual-fidelity problem rates and quantitative performance are partially independent axes: MedGemma 1.5 concentrates both, but Qwen3-VL shows a nearly as high problem rate with quantitative performance much closer to Gemini's. The CNN's Grad-CAM maps, in turn, had their human review -- covering all 40 maps selected for this exploratory analysis (ten per confusion-matrix cell), with no adjudication against the AI-assisted reading -- disagree with that reading in 65\% of cases; this result follows a different design (visual vs.\ textual criteria) and is not directly comparable to the synthesis review's rate. The multi-agent system is proposed as support for non-specialist physicians, under a conceptual use scenario not yet validated with professionals in real consultations. Taken together, the results characterize, in proportion to the evidence gathered, specific trade-offs between metrics, dependent on the model and condition evaluated, not the unconditional superiority of any single approach.

\textbf{Keywords: Melanoma detection, Multi-agent system, Multimodal large language models, Automatic image segmentation, Comparative evaluation, HAM10000.}

\end{resumo}
